Hi all,

Taking a break from staring at fold scores, I made three little paper-cutout animations about this competition. Partly for fun, partly because when I joined I had to google half the labels.

**Ep. 1: Twelve Little Troubles: what the 12 findings actually are.**
[Link](https://www.kaggle.com/code/nilsfl/twelve-little-troubles-a-60-second-knee-film)

**Ep. 2: Inside the Machine: the technical side: slices, 2.5D input, CNN + attention, report labels, folds and ensembles.**
[Link](https://www.kaggle.com/code/nilsfl/inside-the-machine-a-60-second-knee-film-ep-2)

**Ep. 3: The Big Vote: what's inside the public 0.943 notebook: six model families, scores turned into ranks, and a mixing recipe per finding.**
[Link](https://www.kaggle.com/code/nilsfl/the-big-vote-a-one-minute-knee-film-ep-3)

If you're new here, ep. 1 is probably the quicker way in than the data page. If you've been here a while, ep. 2 won't teach you anything, but maybe the robot makes you smile. Ep. 3 is for everyone who forked the 0.943 stack and wondered what all those cells actually do. The notebook below the video has a table with the real weights and slice counts.

To be upfront: voices, music and drawings were generated with open models (eps. 1 and 2 on my laptop, ep. 3 on a cloud GPU). The animation itself is hand-written JavaScript. No competition images were used; the "MRIs" are made from 1918 Gray's Anatomy plates.

They're one-minute cartoons, so things are simplified (the ep. 2 and ep. 3 notebooks list where). If a radiologist here sees something that makes them wince, please tell me and I'll fix it ;)

Good luck in the last weeks!
